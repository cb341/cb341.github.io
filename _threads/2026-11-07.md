[Sparks of Weight-Space Programming](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab, MIT CSAIL, 02.11.2026). GPT-6 Astra reads raw neural network weights and names what they were trained for, and writes small game-playing networks from scratch, without gradients. it edits 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B and MATH-500 goes 7.9% → 48.1% ([logs](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs), [model](https://huggingface.co/RikiyaT/Qwen3.5-0.8B-weight-space-programming)). [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/) puts this kind of self-understanding at late 2027, and its authors [grade reality](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/grading-ai-2027s-2025-predictions) at 70–90% of the scenario's pace. Let's revisit three scenarios:

**I. bloom**: universal income. thinking a full day without consequences. nobody needs my code or my proofs, but i can still write them. process survives, being needed doesn't.

**II. neutral**: the one i actually fear. not unemployment. a hollow job.
the machine does the work. a human signs off.
not because they understand it. not because they’re needed for competence.
because someone has to be liable.
someone who can be blamed. someone who can be shouted at when things go wrong.
you cannot blame anthropic for a 500. you cannot blame openai for a dead patient.
the vendor won’t take the blame, so a human must.
lawyer. doctor. accountant. engineer. the work stays. the process changes.
very high competition to be the meat proxy.

**III. doom**: no jobs to worry about, because there's nothing left at all. not a necessarily a relief, just an end.

every automation before this took something below thinking, and we moved up a level. this time it's thinking itself. is there anything above?

_do i like writing code, or do i like being needed for it?_
