# Chess tactics and endgame vocabulary would otherwise exclude readers new to the game.
glossary:
  blitz:
    definition: Fast chess, commonly three or five minutes per player; an increment adds a few seconds after each move.
  Elo:
    definition: A rating system updating relative playing strength from results compared with expected scores against each opponent’s rating.
  pin:
    definition: A chess attack constraining a piece because moving it would expose a more valuable target behind it.
  skewer:
    definition: A chess attack on a valuable piece that exposes a piece behind it when it moves.
  hanging piece:
    definition: A piece that can be captured without sufficient protection or compensation.
  passed pawn:
    definition: A pawn with no opposing pawn ahead on its file or an adjacent file.
  prophylaxis:
    definition: In chess, anticipating and preventing the opponent's threats.
  opposition:
    definition: Kings facing across one square; the side able to make the other king move first can gain entry.
  square rule:
    definition: A geometric shortcut using an imaginary square to determine whether a king can catch an advancing passed pawn.
  material:
    definition: The pieces and pawns each side has, assessed by their approximate values when comparing captures or exchanges.
  principle of two weaknesses:
    definition: Creating a second target far from the first forces a defender to divide attention and may overload their pieces.
  Lucena position:
    definition: A winning rook-and-pawn versus rook setup where the stronger side builds a rook shield against checks to promote.
    aliases: [lucena]
  Philidor position:
    definition: 'A drawing rook-and-pawn versus rook setup: the defender blocks the king on the sixth rank, then checks from behind.'
    aliases: [philidor]

then found GM José Raúl Capablanca.

everyone learns by memorizing openings. hundreds of variations.
capablanca reduced the entire game to 7 principles:

1. **check for hanging pieces every move**: yours and theirs. before anything else. loose pieces drop off. most games below 1500 are decided by one side leaving a piece undefended, not by deep strategy
2. **activate your king, restrict opponent's king**: centralize yours, cut off theirs. in the endgame the king is a fighting piece
3. **prioritize passed pawns**: main offensive plan is to create and advance them. a passed pawn must be pushed
4. **rule of trade-offs**: winning? trade pieces, keep pawns. losing? trade pawns, keep pieces, seek draw
5. **prevent opponent plans (prophylaxis)**: determine what they want, stop it before pursuing your own
6. **attack weaknesses, principle of two weaknesses**: one weakness rarely loses. create a second on the other side of the board, stretch the defense until it tears
7. **know basic endgames cold**: king+pawn vs king, opposition, square rule, lucena, philidor. without these the rest is theatre

bishop endgames add some more:

* place your pawns on **opposite color** to your bishop
* force opponent's pawns onto **same color** as their bishop
* restricts their bishop, creates permanent targets, maximizes yours

the more i learn, the more there is.
but capablanca shows it can still be thought about simply.
which is very elegant to me.

i realized that i already do this instinctively.
not with rules but with board state.
